# Private AI Product Vision

## Long-term vision

Build a private AI collaborator for individuals and organizations that understands their environment, identifies where it can help, and independently works to become more useful within boundaries they set. People explain their goals in ordinary conversation, grant access to relevant information, and judge results on work they care about. The AI handles choices about models, training data, and workflows.

The collaborator can notice problems and opportunities, investigate them using authorized resources, ask useful questions, and propose projects. For example, a maintenance team can ask it to learn about its machinery and improve its troubleshooting. The AI can study authorized manuals and past cases, spot conflicting instructions, and test whether a better way of finding the right revision improves its advice.

## Learning and evaluation

Learning means becoming demonstrably better at a task the owner values. The AI should select and compare methods suited to the goal, such as improving retrieval, organizing knowledge, maintaining approved memory, learning from feedback, refining tools and workflows, or adapting a model. It should consider the expected benefit, resource cost, and permissions for each approach.

Evaluate candidate changes on representative tasks using checks designed independently of the improvement process. Compare them with the current version, look for regressions, and report uncertainty or failed attempts honestly. Version changes so the owner can reject or roll them back. Schedule authorized background work within resource limits and keep everyday assistance available whenever practical.

## Privacy and authority

Core assistance and learning should work offline. The owner controls access to data and learned knowledge, including what the AI can keep or forget. Any future use of external services requires an explicit, scoped decision about what data may leave the installation.

The AI may organize authorized information, run approved evaluations, and carry out low risk, reversible work under standing permissions. People can review, pause, or revoke that authority. Sensitive access, consequential actions, and material system changes require appropriate authorization. Make significant actions and changes inspectable, and notify people when a finding or decision warrants their attention.

For a personal installation, the individual who controls the device and its data sets the boundaries. In an organization, authorized administrators and users exercise authority on the organization's behalf. Design organizational permissions with distinct controls for shared knowledge, individual privacy, audit access, and revocation.

## Product strategy and current MVP

The personal home appliance is the starting point. The long-term audience also includes small businesses, engineering firms, legal practices, and eventually larger organizations. Product management will use customer validation and technical feasibility to choose which users, problems, and capabilities to pursue next.

The [project brief](PROJECT_BRIEF.md) defines the current MVP around home setup, trusted devices, and local chat and document question answering. The [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) sets the delivery sequence and exit criteria. Organizational features and autonomous improvement are later capabilities. Preserve a path to them through replaceable inference and explicit permission boundaries where those choices also serve the current product. Select hardware, models, and learning methods based on evidence.

The [manual revision experiment](LEARNING_EXPERIMENT.md) describes how to test an early part of the learning vision with synthetic data.
